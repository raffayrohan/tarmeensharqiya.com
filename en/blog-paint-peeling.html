Paint peeling in coastal homes: diagnose the problem by its shape

Published 2026-11-12 — the Sharqia Paints & Decor team

If you repaint the same wall every year, the problem is not the paint — it is what lies beneath it. The shape of the damage itself tells you the cause, and this is the diagnosis table we use on inspections:

Damage shapeMost likely causeCure
Small bubbles & blistersPainting over a damp wall, or high heat during applicationScrape the area, dry, prime, repaint
Peeling in large sheets exposing plasterSalt and rising damp from the baseScrape to sound material + salt treatment and sealing
Fine web-like cracksPutty applied too thick in one pass, or paint over undried puttySand and re-putty in thin, dried layers
Yellowing & stainsWater leak from plumbing or roofFix the source first — paint is the last step

Why does “paint over old paint” fail?

Because the new layer inherits the old one's weakness: any trapped humidity or salt beneath it keeps pushing until both fall together. On our painting projects, preparation takes longer than the painting itself — and that is the result that lasts.

Prevention is cheaper than cure

  • Keep furniture 5cm away from north walls so air circulates.
  • Fix any plumbing leak as soon as it appears, however small.
  • Choose profile-class facade systems for sea-facing elevations.
  • Avoid painting at peak summer humidity if possible — the best season for exterior painting in the Eastern Province is November to March.
